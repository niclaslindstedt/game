---
title: A ninth section is where the nav stops fitting at ANY width — check that it wrapped rather than that it fits
date: 2026-07-31
scope: pwa/scripts/library/
concepts: [navigation, layout, wrapping]
---

The existing lesson about the nav ("the nav's last item vanishes without a
trace when a section is added") has a second act. Adding the ninth name took
the nav past the point where it fits on one line beside the brand at ANY
viewport width: measured across 880–1700 px, it is two right-aligned rows
everywhere above the 900 px burger breakpoint.

That is not a regression, and reading it as one is the trap. Stage 2 of the
nav's own three-stage giving-way (styles.mjs) is exactly this: it wraps within
itself rather than clipping or making the page scroll sideways. Nothing is lost,
nothing scrolls, and the whole cost is **8 px of sticky header** (56 → 64),
because the second row is short and the header's padding already absorbs most
of it. Two tidy rows beat one clipped one, as the original lesson says.

Three things follow:

- **Measure before you "fix" it.** The instinct is to raise the burger
  breakpoint so the burger covers every width where the names do not fit — which
  now means burger everywhere, i.e. taking a visible nav away from desktop
  readers of eight existing sections to solve 8 px.
- **The comment in `styles.mjs` is the thing that actually rots.** It said "the
  six names sit beside the brand on one line from about 915 px up" long after
  there were eight. A stale measurement in a comment is worse than none: the
  next person sizes a decision against a number that has not been true for three
  sections. Re-measure and rewrite it whenever you add one.
- **The question for a TENTH section is whether it pushes a THIRD row**, not
  whether it fits on the first. That is the number worth measuring next time,
  and the panel's `max-height` is the thing that has to hold on a phone held
  sideways.

The measurement is ten lines of Playwright: step the viewport width, count the
distinct `getBoundingClientRect().top` values among `.site-nav a`, and read
`.site-head`'s own height beside it.
