title: A caption that leans on an object owes it a STAGED beat, and a capability the plot rides owes one flat sentence where it first strains
date: 2026-08-15

Two failures with the same shape, both found by a player reading the shipped
prelude rather than by any test.

**A prop the caption asserts must be a prop the player SAW.** The prelude closed
on "SHE TOOK HER JACKET" while Ada had been wearing that jacket on the couch the
whole scene and there was nothing in the room to take. It reads as a line about
nothing, and every downstream beat that leans on the jacket ("it's still
answering") inherits the confusion. The fix is staging, not words: a two-state
prop on one mark, a `move` that stops SHORT of it so she stands beside rather
than in front of the thing being taken, and `prop`+`prop`+`pose` collapsing on
one tick. Then the caption is describing something the player watched.

**A mechanism the campaign navigates by must be NAMED once, in the hero's own
flat voice.** "The one I fixed the zipper on" is a fact about a boyfriend; the
five levels after it treat the jacket as a transmitter. Say the mechanism where
the caption already is — and say the RANGE where the range first strains, not at
the moment the device is introduced. An Earth-made tracker heard from the moon
is the question the moon's own arrival monologue should answer, in one sentence,
as an aside about the antagonist's infrastructure. One sentence, once; nothing
later may explain it again, or the aside becomes a subplot.

The test for both: read every later line that names the thing and ask what a
player who has only seen the prelude makes of it. `story.md` and the manuscript
will happily agree with each other and with the data while the SCENE explains
nothing — the three-tier check is for drift between tiers, not for whether tier
one is legible on its own.
