---
title: Before a giver names a thing, check who was in the room — the ASK is a kind, the hero names the particular
date: 2026-08-12
scope: content/quests/, content/conversations/
concepts: [dialogue, continuity, quest-offer, point-of-view]
---

Ruth's first errand had her instruct the hero with a fact only he and Ada
possessed: "SHE BOUGHT CHIPS AND A SODA THAT NIGHT, AND THE MACHINES PRINT A
SLIP." She was not in that house. Nothing in the build catches it — the schema
checks page length, not point of view — and it reads as flat exposition even
before you notice it is impossible.

The shape that fixes it is reusable, because a quest offer is a two-page scene
(`offer:` then a `- hero:` page): **the giver asks for a KIND of thing and the
hero supplies the particular.** Ruth asks "WHAT DID SHE GO OUT FOR?"; he answers
"CHIPS AND A SODA. THE MACHINES PRINT A SLIP." The errand still points at
exactly one item, and the giver never claims knowledge they cannot have.

Two things fall out of it for free:

- **The handover becomes a discovery.** "CHIPS AND HER SODA, 11:52 PM. 'PAYMENT
  INTERRUPTED.'" was Ruth restating her own premise; now it is her reading the
  slip and finding out. Same words, better beat.
- **The `incomplete:` line should cite the hero, not the world.** "SOMEBODY ON
  THAT FLOOR IS CARRYING IT" re-asserts the knowledge; "YOU SAID THEY CARRY THE
  PAPERWORK" hands it back to him.

Ruth's other two errands already had the right shape and were left alone: THE
DENT is an explicit prediction ("THEY'LL HAVE GIVEN HER… SHE'LL HAVE BITTEN
IT"), and THE SCALE relays what the hero came home and told her. Check every
giver line against a chain of custody before rewriting one.
