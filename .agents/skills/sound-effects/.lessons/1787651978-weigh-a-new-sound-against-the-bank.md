---
title: A new sound is authored for how it sounds ALONE — weigh its peak against the bank's loudest before shipping it
date: 2026-08-25
scope: content/sounds/
concepts: [mixing, verification, layering, limiter]
---

A sound written on its own is written for how it sounds on its own, which is
never how it is heard. Nothing in the pipeline notices: it compiles, its ids
resolve, and it arrives in the game as the loudest thing in the mix, riding the
master limiter and flattening everything under it.

Render it beside the bank's current heavyweight — `rocket_boom_deep`, the
deepest explosion the game has, peak ~0.13 — and place the new one against that
number ON PURPOSE. The flight's launch pair first came out at 0.19 / 0.21 peak,
half again louder than the biggest bang in the game, and was trimmed to sit
just above it: the biggest moment in the game, and no more than that.

Two sounds authored to LAYER (an ignition, then the lift-off over its tail) are
weighed as the SUM they arrive as, not one at a time — the second one's whole
job is to add what the first does not have, so its own peak is only half the
question.

The renderer is the one described in the sibling lesson on judging a ladder
offline; the extra column worth printing for this is simply peak, beside two or
three existing sounds you already know the loudness of.
