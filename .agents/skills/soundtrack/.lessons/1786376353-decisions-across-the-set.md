---
title: When refreshing a WHOLE soundtrack, the binding constraint is that no two tracks may share a decision — otherwise you get nine tempos, not nine places
date: 2026-08-10
scope: content/songs/, content/music/
concepts: [arrangement, composition, review]
---

SKILL.md says a track's character is two or three decisions. Doing every score
in one pass adds a constraint it does not: **the decisions have to be distinct
ACROSS the catalogue.** The obvious ideas converge fast — "a semitone shift",
"a chromatic fall", "a drone", "a pulse that never stops" are each strong once
and mush the fourth time, and the failure is invisible track-by-track because
each one still reads well on its own sheet.

What worked: write the decision list for the whole set BEFORE composing any of
it, then assign — a sinking semitone to the office, a chromatic fall to the
landlords, an unmoving drone to the moon, no tonic at all to the rift, an
inescapable ground bass to the vault. The table now lives in `GAME_NOTES.md`
and is the first thing a tenth track has to be checked against.

The same rule settles the "should I rewrite this one?" question honestly.
`long_noon` was left alone because its decisions were already distinct and
already documented; a rewrite would have been churn. A track whose decisions are
strong and unique is finished, whatever the brief said.

**And the brief's adjective is not the genre.** "Sanctuary" was written as a
music-box lullaby in C major and rejected outright on the first listen — sweet,
twee, and about a character the game does not have. The garage is quiet because
nothing is hunting him, not because he is tender: he drives through a crowd at a
hundred and twenty and files it under road surface. The second take asked "what
is this man DOING in this room" instead of "what does this room feel like", got
a workshop groove with a bass hook, and was right.

The transferable check: name the person in the room and what their hands are
busy with, THEN pick the genre. A brief word like sanctuary, dread or awe
describes the player's experience and picks no instrument at all — taking it as
a genre cue is how a hub theme ends up as a nursery.
