---
title: When a sprite's own `subject` names the defect, the WORDS are what you fix first
date: 2026-07-30
scope: content/sprites/
concepts: [subject-field, descriptions, regeneration]
---

Phase 4 step 1 asks whether a pass is changing WHAT a sprite is or just drawing
it better. There is a loud tell: read the generated acceptance prompt and see
whether the words themselves describe the problem.

THE RIFT's voidling was "a small, ragged, lumpy formless blob" and its unraveler
"a lumpy vertical blob, an unstable smear". Those are not targets a good sprite
could hit — "formless blob" IS the blob-syndrome defect, written down and
shipped. Grounding concepts on that prompt would have reproduced the defect with
tidier pixels.

So: if the subject names a shape that the rubric would score down (formless,
lumpy, a smear, a blob), that sprite's WORDS are the first edit. Rewrite
`description`/`subject` from the manuscript and the def's `lore` — which is
where the real design usually already is (the manuscript said THE UNRAVELERS
PULL THINGS APART; the voidling's lore said "a scrap of hungry dark … the way a
river is full of silt") — regenerate the prompt, and only then sketch.

The inverse is the common case and needs no ceremony: when the words are good
and the pixels ignore them (a "jagged faceted crystal" drawn as a rounded crown,
"a spiky radiating corona" drawn as a speckled ball), it is pure fidelity — keep
the prompt, fix the grid.
