---
type: Added
title: Co-op rules — whose XP, whose loot, and what a body nobody is steering means
---

A multiplayer session now shares out a kill the way Diablo 2 does. XP from a
kill goes to the heroes who were near enough to have been in the fight, split in
proportion to level — so a low-level friend playing with a veteran gains far
more than they could alone (the horde is priced against the party's highest
level), while the veteran is not taxed for bringing them along. An arrow, a
handed-in errand or a scripted award still belongs to whoever earned it.

Loot is **free for all** by default — anything on the floor goes to whoever
reaches it. A host who would rather not run that race can set HOST GAME → LOOT
to **ALLOCATED**, and every drop is then set aside for one of the players who
was in the fight.

A player who **leaves** no longer leaves a problem behind. Their hero stops
counting: the horde ignores it, it stops holding the difficulty up over the
people still playing, its seat is offered to the next person to join, and — the
one that mattered — the party can now be defeated. Before this, a group whose
fourth player quit could be wiped over and over without the run ever ending.

The escalation meter now reads a party's combat output per player rather than
as one enormous hero, so a group of four fighting at an ordinary pace is judged
as an ordinary fight instead of provoking a permanent rampage inside the first
minute.

None of this changes a single-player run.
