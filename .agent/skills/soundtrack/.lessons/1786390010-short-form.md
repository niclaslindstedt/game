OVERDUE and AN HOUR BEHIND were seventy-six-bar, ten-section, ~110 s loops
written to the same shape as the level themes. A leg of the DRIVE is over in
40–60 s. So their entire back half — breakdown, build, turn — had never been
heard by anybody, and nothing in the tree said so: every test was green, every
sheet read well, and the fault is invisible section-by-section because each
section is fine. **The question a score answers is "how long will this be
playing", and it is not asked anywhere in the format.**

Measure it rather than guessing. `make drive-bench` reports the leg's duration
per rung (the auto-driver's ~110 s is the CAUTIOUS end — a competent human is
half that, which is why the user's 40–60 s and the bench's number are both
right). For a level, the answer is minutes and the two-minute loop is correct.

Three things the short form does differently, and they generalize:

- **THE BUILD BECOMES A HINGE.** Eight bars of climb is a fifth of a fifty-second
  piece spent going up. Four bars, twice as steep, played TWICE — into the
  chorus and into the turn — gives a 36-bar loop two peaks instead of one.
- **THE INTRO DOES THE BREAKDOWN'S JOB.** There is no room to go thin in the
  middle, so the thin moment is bar 1 (which is also the bar the title card sits
  on). Both road scores now open on the one voice their breakdown used to expose.
- **THE SIGNATURE ARRIVES EARLY.** Written for a listener who gets ONE pass, not
  one who will hear a restatement — OVERDUE's siren enters in bar 5 of A.

The bound lives in `tests/chiptune_test.ts` as a `SHORT_FORM` set of ids, so a
new minigame score joins that set and a level's never does.
